What am I looking at?

The banners

Nothing appears here on a quiet day. Two kinds can show up:

QPE/FFG · ARI
a confirmation signal. Across three verified events the high thresholds produced no false alarms in 136 samples, but detected only about a tenth of them — so a banner means what the depth score already shows is climatologically extreme, never that something new has been found.
Cell approaching
an intense, persistent cell closing on the basin on a day whose environment supports storms. It states position and heading only. It does not estimate rainfall or basin response.

The rose

The centre is the basin boundary collapsed to a point, and the rings are distance from that boundary — not from the watershed centre. The boundary itself lies 8–12 mi out from the midpoint depending on direction, so add roughly that much when comparing against rings drawn around a centroid on a map.

dot
a detected cell, at its true compass bearing and range. Size scales with area.
line
the cell's own heading, length scaled to speed. Pointing inward means closing.
amber
closing on the basin
blue
receding or moving across
sage
detected, but not matched to the previous frame
dashed ring
the match is suspect — see Flags below

The rose shows the current cycle only. The two panels beside it cover 48 hours.

The table

Time
which radar frame the row came from. Two frame pairs are written each cycle, so rows span two windows — newest first, with a rule at the change. A feature that appears in both with a consistent heading is far more trustworthy than one that appears once.
Product
rate is MRMS PrecipRate, the instantaneous surface rainfall rate. refl is the reflectivity composite, which sees the storm aloft and may pick up a cell before it produces rain at the ground — the reason both are logged.
Position
where the cell sits relative to the basin boundary.
Moving
the cell's own heading and speed. Not the same as Position.
Closing
the component of that motion pointed at the basin. Positive is approaching; a cell moving fast across the front of the basin closes slowly.
ETA
range divided by closing speed. Hidden past 3 hours, where it is arithmetic rather than a forecast.
Cell
footprint area and peak intensity.

Flags

reversed
heading against the mean flow of every other cell, at above ambient speed.
overspeed
far faster than everything else in the same airmass, even where the direction looks reasonable.
new / decayed
could not be paired with the previous frame. Expected for a cell that has just formed, and not an error.

Both flags are the signature of one cell decaying while another forms nearby and the two being paired by mistake. Cells advect with roughly the same steering flow, so a lone reversal or a lone sprinter is almost always bookkeeping rather than weather. Flagged rows are still logged in full; only the display greys their closing speed and ETA.

What this is

Phase 1, log-only. Nothing here changes a threat tier, a footprint, an alert or anything on the operational dashboard. It exists to accumulate a season of observed values so that thresholds can be set from data rather than guessed — including quiet cycles, which are what make a false-alarm rate computable at all.

The stationary speed bin is the hazard case, not the safe one: a cell parked over a drainage is the classic Moab flood signature, so a future rule must never read "not moving" as "stand down".

Display units are imperial. The log stays metric, because that is what MRMS publishes and what the season analysis should run on.
